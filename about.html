Our story

We got tired of stays that photograph
better than they feel.

Founded 2016

Ten years, eighteen cabins, one rule.

Fernhollow started with a single A-frame in the Catskills that a founder rebuilt by hand over one long winter. The rule was simple then and hasn't changed: every stay has to be a real, single place with its own personality: not a unit in a complex, not a listing template.

A decade later that's still true of all eighteen stays in the collection. These days we add a few most years rather than waiting between each one, but the bar hasn't moved: every stay still has to be worth the wait.

What we care about

Three things we won't compromise on.

01

Real privacy

No shared walls, no shared driveways where we can help it. Every stay is a single, whole building.

02

Honest photos

What you see in the gallery is what you get: same lens, same light, no staging tricks.

03

A real human host

Every stay has one dedicated host who actually knows the property, not a call centre.

How a stay makes the cut

Four questions every listing has to survive.

Most applications don't make it past question two.

Is it actually private?

No shared walls or driveways where we can help it, and enough distance from the road or neighbors that it feels like yours.

Would we stay a second night?

A place can photograph beautifully and still be uncomfortable to actually live in for three days. We check both.

Is the host actually reachable?

Every host has to commit to a real response window before a listing goes live, and we check in on that after the first few bookings.

Does it hold up in bad weather?

A stay has to be genuinely good on a cold, rainy Tuesday, not just on the sunny weekend it was photographed.

Ten years, roughly

How the collection grew.

2016

The first cabin

One A-frame in the Catskills, booked directly through a spreadsheet and a shared inbox.

2019

Six stays

Word of mouth carried the first few years; six carefully chosen stays across four states.

2023

A real booking flow

Retired the shared inbox for a proper browse-to-confirmation flow, the one this site demonstrates.

Early 2026

Twelve stays

Six new regions added, from Maine to Montana, each held to the same four questions as the first.

Today

Eighteen stays

Six more, from Texas Hill Country to the Idaho Sawtooths, still held to the same bar.

A note from the team

"We'd rather have eighteen stays we're proud of than sixty we're not."

That's the whole pitch, really. Every property in the collection is one that the team has personally stayed in, more than once, in every season. If a place doesn't hold up to a rainy Tuesday in March as well as a sunny long weekend in July, it doesn't make the collection.
